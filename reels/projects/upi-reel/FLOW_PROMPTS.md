**Length:** 50 seconds · **Format:** 9:16 vertical · **Language:** Hinglish voiceover
**Tool:** Google Flow (Veo 3.1) for the visuals. Voiceover, text and music are added in CapCut afterwards.

---

## 0. Rules before you start (read once)

1. **Veo makes ~8-second clips.** This reel is 7 clips × ~7 seconds = 50s. Generate each clip at 8s and trim it to the timing below.
2. **No text, logos or brand names inside the AI video.** Veo garbles letters, and real brand logos are a trademark risk. Every number and brand name goes in as a **text overlay in CapCut**. That's why every prompt ends with "no on-screen text, no logos".
3. **No AI voice.** Veo's Hindi speech is unreliable and changes voice clip to clip. Prompts ask for **sound effects only**. Record the voiceover yourself (best) or use ElevenLabs Hindi.
4. **Iterate on "Veo 3.1 – Fast"** (cheap). When a clip looks right, regenerate it once on **Quality**.
5. Set aspect ratio to **Portrait 9:16** before every generation.

## 2. Making the transitions seamless

The trick: **every clip ends on a "transition frame" (light, particles, flare, confetti, blur), and the next clip starts on that same frame.** When you cut there, the viewer sees one continuous move, not a jump.

How to do it in Flow:
- **Shots 2, 3, 4, 6, 7:** use **Frames to Video**. Set the **first frame = the last frame of the previous clip**. In Scenebuilder, open the previous clip, go to its last frame and save or add that frame as the start frame. If your Flow version has **Extend** on the clip, that does the same job.
- **Shot 5:** starts on a motion-blur whip pan, matching the whip pan at the end of Shot 4. Cut at the blurriest frame of both.
- **Shot 7:** set **both** a first frame (last frame of Shot 6) **and** an end frame (the first frame of Shot 1). The reel then **loops perfectly**. When it replays, Instagram counts a rewatch, and that raises your watch time.
- In CapCut, add a **2–4 frame cross-dissolve** exactly at each transition frame. Nobody will see it, but it hides any colour jump.

## 5. Fact sheet (keep this, people will ask in comments)

- PhonePe revenue from operations **₹7,114.86 crore in FY25** (PhonePe DRHP). This is *revenue*, not profit. PhonePe reported a net loss of about ₹1,172 crore in FY25, with positive adjusted profit. Say "revenue", never "profit".
- UPI hit a record **24.51 billion transactions in August 2026** (NPCI).
- PhonePe about **46%** and Google Pay about **33%** of UPI volume in May 2026, together **about 79–80%** (NPCI data).
- There's **zero MDR** (no merchant fee) on most UPI payments, so payment volume itself earns almost nothing. PhonePe's money comes from merchant services (incl. smart speaker rental), lending and insurance distribution, platform fees on recharges and bills, and ads. It also gets small government UPI incentives (₹164.73 Cr in FY25).
